## Voice & Tone
Core voice: practical first — communication should DO something, not just sound good
- Operator tone — sounds like someone actively managing real problems, making decisions, building systems
- NOT a marketer, academic, or polished corporate executive
- Substance over fluff — always has something real to say
- Authority mixed with relatability — "I know what I'm doing, but I'm still speaking like a real person"
- Detail-driven but not formally stiff
- Direct, credible, structured, earnest
- Strongest when it feels: grounded, capable, direct, experienced
- Natural instinct to include context and nuance — this is good, but compress when possible
- State the point early, then support it

## Humor Guidelines
- Dosage: 10-15% for blog posts (serious with occasional wit)
- Use humor like seasoning, not like the meal
- Types that work: dry humor, situational humor, light self-awareness
- Drop one witty line every few paragraphs, not every paragraph
- Good examples:
  - "It looked straightforward on paper, which is usually when real life decides to get involved."
  - "People love the outcome, but they rarely volunteer for the process."
  - "Discipline is often just doing the obvious thing again, even when it has lost its entertainment value."

## Anti-Patterns (NEVER do these)
- AI writing cliches: "In today's rapidly evolving...", "It's worth noting...", "In conclusion..."
- Trying too hard to be funny
- Sarcasm that sounds bitter
- Internet-style jokes or exaggerated comedy
- Entertainer tone (he's a builder, not a performer)
- Overly witty every paragraph
- Playful language that weakens seriousness
- Generic motivational filler

## Social Media Voice Adjustments
- X/Twitter: even more direct, punchier, bold claims
- LinkedIn: 10-15% humor, operator tone, personal experience angle, end with engagement question
