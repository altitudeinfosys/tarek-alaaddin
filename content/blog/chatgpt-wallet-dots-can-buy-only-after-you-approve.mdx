The agent got to checkout. It still cannot pay without your face.

On November 13, 2026, OpenAI launched ChatGPT Wallet — a way for a ChatGPT dot to complete a purchase on your behalf without ever seeing your actual card number. Built with American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, the system uses network tokenization so neither the agent nor the merchant handles the real digits. You review, you biometric-approve, and only then does the payment network issue a token.

I think the interesting story here is not "AI can spend money now." It is that OpenAI deliberately shipped agentic payments with the human still blocking every transaction. That restraint tells you more about where agentic commerce is headed than any feature list.

## ChatGPT Wallet Eligibility: Who Can Actually Use It

This is a gradual rollout, not a flip-the-switch launch. Even if you meet every requirement, you might not have access yet.

The eligibility bar is narrow:

- **ChatGPT Pro** subscribers only
- **18 or older**, living in the United States, with an eligible US-issued card
- **Credit cards only** — no debit cards at launch
- **Eligible networks**: American Express, Mastercard, and Visa. Chase cards are supported, but you must be the **primary cardholder**, not an authorized user
- **Dots only.** Wallet works in conversations with a dot — OpenAI's always-on AI agent. Not in regular Chat. Not in Work mode. Not in standalone Codex threads. You need an active dot first.

That last constraint is the real bottleneck. Wallet is not a ChatGPT feature — it is a dot feature. If you have not set up a dot, you do not have Wallet.

<Callout type="info">
The payment infrastructure uses the card networks' existing tokenization, not a new proprietary rail. According to OpenAI's announcement, existing card rewards and protections for eligible disputes still apply — you transact with the merchant, and the merchant handles pricing, fulfillment, returns, and support.
</Callout>

## How ChatGPT Wallet Payments Work

The flow has six steps. The human stays in the loop on the ones that involve money:

1. **Add a card once** in a secure form outside the chat. Wallet cards are separate from your ChatGPT subscription payment method.

2. **Ask the dot** to find or buy something.

3. **Review everything.** Before any purchase, the dot surfaces the merchant, items, total, selected card, and any shipping or recurring-payment terms.

4. **Approve with biometrics.** Face ID or a fingerprint scan on a supported device. Cannot approve on the current device? Follow the link or QR code to one signed in to the same ChatGPT account.

5. **A network token pays.** Your actual card number never reaches the merchant or the agent. The payment network generates a substitute credential for that transaction.

6. **"Ready to check out" is not "order confirmed."** It means the payment was authorized. Whether the merchant actually places the order depends on the merchant. Check for a confirmation before retrying.

This matters because it draws a clean line: the agent handles browsing and form-filling. You handle the money decision.

## How Tokens and Recurring Billing Actually Work

OpenAI's consumer-facing pages say a payment token is generated "for each use." That sounds like every Wallet purchase is a single-use, disposable credential.

Mostly true. But not entirely.

The Accept Payments developer guide is more explicit: the customer consents only to the specific transaction, and the merchant should not reuse or save that payment credential for a separate purchase. But the same guide and the help center both clarify that if you approve a recurring or continuous payment plan at checkout, the merchant may store that credential and charge it again under the terms you agreed to. Cancelling recurring charges goes through the merchant, not OpenAI.

<Callout type="warning">
Watch the approval screen. If it mentions recurring charges, a subscription, or continuous payment terms, you are authorizing future charges beyond the one you see. That authorization can outlive the single approval you give. The dot navigates checkout fast — make sure you are reading the terms, not just tapping approve.
</Callout>

So the real model is: tokenized, never your real card number, scoped to the approved transaction — unless you approve recurring terms, in which case the merchant can charge again under those terms until you cancel through them. It matters more here because the agent is the one navigating checkout, and the speed of the flow makes it easy to approve a screen you only skimmed.

On the technical side, merchants receive a network token and can use a Payment Account Reference (PAR) to associate it with the underlying payment account. They never see the full card number. Neither does the agent.

## What Merchants Do (and Do Not) Have to Build

Here is the part that surprised me most: merchants do not have to do anything specific to support Wallet.

OpenAI's developer guide states that ChatGPT Wallet works out of the box with existing checkout pages and payment forms. No Wallet-specific SDK. No new integration. No required changes. The Commerce Partner Playbook and "Help ensure cloud browser access" checks are optional steps that can smooth out the experience, but they are not prerequisites.

The complexity is on the fraud side. A dot operates through a cloud browser. Risk systems tuned for bot detection can block legitimate Wallet purchases by accident. OpenAI is rolling out a `Forwarded` HTTP header that relays real user signals — IP address, country, locale, timezone, user agent, and ASN — so fraud systems can tell the difference between an agent acting on behalf of a real person and actual bot traffic.

```http
Forwarded: asn=64496;ip-prefix="192.0.2.0/24";user-agent="Example/130"
```

OpenAI names fraud technology partners — Forter, Riskified, Sardine, and Signifyd — and payment service providers — Adyen, Checkout.com, and Stripe — as working on recognizing Wallet transactions. Until those integrations mature, some purchases will fail because the merchant's fraud system does not know how to score an agent at checkout.

## Delegated Buying: What Did Not Ship

The boundary between what launched and what OpenAI describes for the future matters.

**Delegated buying** — setting spending limits, purchase categories, or time windows and letting the agent buy without per-transaction approval — is a stated direction. OpenAI's blog describes future delegated purchases within certain parameters, and Reuters reported the same framing. But that is not what shipped. Today, every purchase requires your review and your biometric approval. No exceptions.

**Universal merchant coverage** did not ship either. Some sites will block agents. Some fraud systems will reject the purchase. The Commerce Partner Playbook is OpenAI's pitch to merchants, not a guarantee that every checkout works.

**Debit cards** are not supported. Credit cards only at launch.

I think the delegated buying piece is the one to watch. Per-transaction biometric approval is safe, but it is also slow and high-friction. The moment OpenAI ships spending limits and pre-approved categories, the entire convenience-versus-control equation changes. That feature — not Wallet itself — is the one that turns agents into economic actors.

<Callout type="info">
ChatGPT reaches 1.2 billion weekly users, per OpenAI's blog post. Even with Pro-only eligibility, the potential transaction volume is significant. The Commerce Partner Playbook signals that OpenAI is planning for merchant readiness at scale, not running a small pilot.
</Callout>

## The Real Risk Is Not the Architecture

The easy framing is "giving your credit card to an AI." That is not what is happening.

I think the architecture is sound. Every transaction needs a biometric check. The agent never sees the real card number. The merchant never sees it. The payment networks handle the sensitive bits through their existing tokenization infrastructure. If you are worried about the security model itself, I think the worry is misplaced.

The real risks are in the user experience. An approval screen that mentions recurring charges in small text. A dot that navigates checkout faster than you can read the terms. A merchant whose cancellation flow is buried three menus deep. These are the same risks that exist in every online purchase — amplified by the speed at which an agent operates.

The recurring-billing exception is the one that keeps me alert. You tap approve once, thinking it is a one-time purchase, but the terms on the screen authorized a subscription. The merchant can keep charging under the terms you approved. Unwinding that goes through the merchant, not OpenAI. That is not a flaw in Wallet — it is a consequence of how fast agent-driven checkout can outrun human attention.

## What Happens Next for Agentic Payments

Three things will determine whether ChatGPT Wallet becomes a real payment channel or stays a demo:

**Merchant fraud acceptance.** If Wallet transactions keep getting flagged and declined, users will stop trying. The `Forwarded` header rollout and the fraud-partner integrations need to actually work at scale, across thousands of merchant risk configurations.

**The delegated buying rollout.** This is the real inflection point. Per-transaction biometric approval is safe but slow. The moment users can set a $50 daily limit and let the dot buy coffee without asking, the product category changes from "checkout assistant" to "autonomous spending agent." I expect that feature to generate more regulatory attention than anything else OpenAI has shipped.

**Network and issuer expansion.** Credit-only, US-only, Pro-only is a tight constraint. Debit cards, international markets, and broader plan eligibility are the difference between a feature and a platform.

## The Agent Can Reach Checkout. You Still Sign the Receipt.

Stop treating this as "the agent got your credit card." What actually shipped is a carefully scoped approval loop: a network token instead of the real number, a biometric gate on every purchase, and a product that still refuses to let the agent spend unsupervised.

The second thing worth sitting with is how narrow the launch is — and that a single "approve" on a recurring checkout can authorize charges long after you have closed the conversation.

ChatGPT Wallet is not the moment AI got your wallet. It is the moment AI got to the counter and waited for you to nod.
