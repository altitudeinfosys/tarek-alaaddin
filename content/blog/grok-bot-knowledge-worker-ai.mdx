On August 11, 2026, SpaceXAI — the entity formed by xAI's [merger with Cursor](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/grok-bot-iphone-mac-ai-agents) in a $60 billion all-stock deal — shipped [Grok Bot](https://x.ai/news/introducing-grok-bot) in early beta. The pitch is five words long: "AI teammates you can give real work to."

That phrasing matters. Not "an AI that answers questions." Not "a chatbot that drafts things for you to review." Teammates you hand work to — the way you would hand it to a new hire who happens to never sleep, never forget a thread, and never need a lunch break.

Most AI tools still live inside a chat window. You ask, it answers, you copy the output somewhere useful. Grok Bot flips that. Each Bot gets its own persistent cloud computer, its own browser, its own file system, and its own logins. You describe a task the way you would text a colleague, then close your laptop. The Bot keeps working. It produces output directly inside the tool being used — an inbox, a CRM, a spreadsheet — not in the chat.

## What Grok Bot Actually Is

Grok Bot is an autonomous AI agent platform that runs on macOS, iOS, Windows, and Linux, with Android coming soon. You interact with it through a messaging interface — think of it as texting an extremely competent colleague rather than prompting a language model.

Each bot operates on dedicated cloud infrastructure. That means your device is irrelevant once the task is assigned. Turn off your phone, shut your MacBook, fly across the country — the Bot is still logged into Salesforce pulling the data you asked for.

The architecture is role-based. Instead of one do-everything assistant, you build specialized bots: one for email triage, one for LinkedIn research, one for deal negotiation, one for expense processing. You can address multiple bots simultaneously using @-mentions, and they coordinate among themselves in group chats — passing work, assigning ownership, and only pulling you in when a judgment call is needed.

<Callout type="info">
**This is not Apple Intelligence or Siri.** The "iMessage AI" framing is tempting because you interact with Grok Bot by messaging it on your iPhone, but it is a standalone app, not an iMessage integration. It is closer to having a team of remote workers you manage through a chat interface than to a system-level AI assistant.
</Callout>

## How It Connects to X — and Why That Matters

Grok is built by xAI, which also built X (formerly Twitter). That gives Grok Bot something no competitor has: native, real-time access to every public conversation happening on the platform.

Setting up X monitoring is a one-click integration from Grok Bot's connection library. No API keys, no OAuth dance, no third-party automation tool in the middle. You tell a Bot to "check my X mentions every 30 minutes and flag anything that needs a reply," and it does — using the same live data feed that powers Grok's trend analysis.

That integration extends beyond monitoring. A Bot can draft replies based on your voice and tone, track competitor accounts, pull trending topics into a brief, or alert you when a conversation about your product hits a threshold. ChatGPT has no access to live X data. Claude does not either. This is a genuine moat, and xAI knows it.

<Callout type="warning">
**The X connection cuts both ways.** Real-time platform access makes Grok Bot uniquely powerful for social intelligence, but it also means your AI agent is operating inside a social network where one bad automated reply can go viral. Set approval gates on anything that posts publicly.
</Callout>

## The Feature That Changes Everything: Teach by Demonstration

Every AI agent product lets you type instructions. Grok Bot lets you show it what to do.

The teach-by-demonstration feature records you performing a workflow once — clicking through Salesforce, filling out a form, navigating an internal tool — and then the Bot generalizes the procedure and repeats it independently. Including on a schedule.

This matters because the hardest automation problems are not the ones with clean APIs. They are the ones locked inside legacy tools that have no API at all. A monitoring dashboard that only works in a browser. An internal HR tool from 2014. An invoicing system that requires four clicks and a dropdown to file a single expense. Grok Bot navigates those the way a person would — because it has its own browser and screen.

One [documented beta use case](https://www.ayautomate.com/blog/grok-bot-xai-ai-agents-explained) showed a Bot learning to pull Beehiiv newsletter statistics by watching a single screen recording. No API integration needed. No Zapier. No code.

## What It Costs — and Whether It's Worth It

Here is the uncomfortable part. Grok Bot has no standalone free plan.

Access requires one of three subscriptions:

| Plan | Cost | Who It's For |
|------|------|-------------|
| SuperGrok Heavy | $300/month | Power users who want the full xAI ecosystem |
| Cursor Ultra | $200/month | Developers using the Cursor IDE |
| Cursor Teams Premium | $120/seat/month | Teams with centralized billing and SSO |

Each plan includes a weekly usage allowance (xAI does not publish the exact size). Overages are billed at Grok 4.6 model rates: [$2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/grok-bot-pricing). There is no spend cap yet.

That is steep. At $120 to $300 per month, evaluation alone is an investment. But consider the math: one beta user described automating a 90-minute sales workflow to roughly 95 percent. If that workflow runs regularly, the subscription pays for itself fast — and every additional Bot you train compounds the return.

The barrier is not the ongoing cost. It is the upfront commitment to figure out which workflows are worth automating before the first month's bill arrives.

## Ten Use Cases That Make a Knowledge Worker Dangerous

These are not hypotheticals. They are drawn from [documented beta use cases](https://latentdigitalsolutions.com/grok-bot-use-cases/) and SpaceXAI's internal testing during launch week.

### 1. Morning Digest That Actually Saves Time

A Bot consolidates your email, Slack, calendar, and call notes into a single daily briefing. Not a summary of everything — a filtered view of what needs your attention, delivered before you open your laptop.

### 2. Inbox Triage on Autopilot

Set a Bot to process incoming email on a schedule you define. It categorizes, flags urgency, drafts replies for routine messages, and escalates only the ones that need your judgment. The output lands in your actual inbox, not in a separate app.

### 3. Sales Outbound in Your Voice

A Bot writes personalized cold emails and LinkedIn outreach using your tone and your data. One beta user described automating a 90-minute sales play to roughly 95% — the Bot does the research, writes the draft, and queues it for review.

### 4. Real-Time X Monitoring

A Bot watches your X mentions, competitor accounts, and industry keywords on a 30-minute cycle. It flags conversations that need attention and drafts responses in your voice. This one leverages the native X integration that no competitor can match.

### 5. Expense Filing from Phone Photos

Snap a photo of a receipt. The Bot reads it, categorizes the expense, and files it with the correct coding in your expense system. No manual entry, no "I'll do it later" pile that grows until quarter-end.

### 6. Job Application Screening at Scale

One beta tester processed 200 applications into strong, mid, and reject categories in a single pass. Another screened over 1,000 event applicants against a target profile with batch approval. The Bot reads applications the way a hiring manager would — except it does not get tired at application number 47.

### 7. Focus Time That Actually Gets Protected

A Bot analyzes your calendar, finds blocks of uninterrupted focus time each week, and flags meetings that fragment deep work. It reads the calendar the same way a good executive assistant would — except it checks every day without being asked.

### 8. CRM Hygiene Without the Drudgery

A Bot logs into Salesforce, checks for stale deals, updates contact records, and monitors pipeline health. One user described answering end-of-quarter CRM questions from a mobile device in roughly ten seconds — because the Bot had already done the work.

### 9. Support Ticket to Documentation Pipeline

A Bot runs regular passes on your support queue, escalating urgent tickets and converting the rest into changelog entries and help articles. The institutional knowledge that normally dies in a closed ticket gets captured automatically.

### 10. Competitive Intelligence on Autopilot

A Bot monitors competitor pricing pages, product updates, and social media mentions. When something changes, you get a structured alert — not a noisy notification, but a brief with context about what changed and why it might matter.

## The Honest Pros and Cons

### What Works

- **Persistent cloud computer per Bot** — work continues when your device is off, which is the architectural difference that separates this from every chat-based agent
- **Teach-by-demonstration** — automate workflows in tools that have no API, which covers most of the real world
- **Native X integration** — real-time social intelligence that ChatGPT and Claude cannot replicate
- **Multi-bot coordination** — specialist agents that divide and delegate without human traffic-directing
- **Cross-platform parity** — the iOS app can do everything the desktop app can, including live session takeover

### What Does Not

- **No model selection** — you cannot choose which model processes your tasks, which is a dealbreaker for compliance-sensitive teams
- **No free tier at launch** — there is a one-time trial on the lower Cursor tier, but full evaluation still requires a $120+ monthly commitment
- **No live voice mode** — only dictation-to-text, no conversational voice interface
- **Beta stability** — internal testers report 2-3x efficiency gains, but "the difference between 90% done and 100% done" still requires human finishing
- **Unpublished usage limits** — the weekly allowance size is not disclosed, making cost planning harder than it should be

<Callout type="info">
**How it compares:** Claude Cowork starts at $20/month and excels at single-agent, long-horizon work like coding and writing. ChatGPT Work (powered by GPT-5.6) leads in agentic workflows with deeper enterprise integrations. Grok Bot's advantage is multi-agent coordination on persistent cloud infrastructure with unmatched X integration — but at a premium price and beta-stage polish.
</Callout>

## Who Should Care

Grok Bot is not for everyone. At $120 to $300 per month, it is not a casual productivity tool. It is infrastructure for people whose time is expensive and whose work involves repetitive, multi-step tasks across multiple apps.

If you spend your days in a rotation of email, CRM, spreadsheets, Slack, and social media — and you find yourself doing the same sequences over and over — this is built for you. The teach-by-demonstration feature alone can automate workflows that no integration platform can touch, because it works with whatever tools you already use, API or not.

If you are a developer who lives in a terminal and a code editor, Cursor's native AI capabilities might serve you better at a lower price point. If you primarily need a writing or research partner, Claude or ChatGPT will get you further for less money.

But if you are a knowledge worker who wants to multiply output without multiplying hours — and you are willing to invest the upfront time to train your Bots properly — Grok Bot is the closest thing the industry has shipped to having an actual AI team.

The question is not whether the technology works. The question is whether your workflows are valuable enough to justify the subscription while xAI figures out the pricing for everyone else.
