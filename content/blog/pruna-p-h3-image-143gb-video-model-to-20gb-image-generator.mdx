A 143 GB video model compressed into a 20 GB image generator that runs in 21 GB of VRAM. That's what Pruna AI shipped on November 3, 2026, and the numbers are real — verified against their [launch blog](https://www.pruna.ai/blog/oss-p-h3-image-launch) and both [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/PrunaAI/P-H3-Image) [model cards](https://huggingface.co/PrunaAI/P-H3-Image-Comfy).

The model is called P-H3-Image, and the engineering behind it is genuinely impressive: distilled encoders, slashed diffusion steps, quantized weights, and a rebuilt decoder — all pulled from MiniMax's open-weights H3 video model and reassembled into something purpose-built for still images.

But there's a catch that changes everything about who can actually use it.

## What MiniMax-H3 Is — and Why It Matters

MiniMax released H3 with open weights earlier this year. It's a 33-billion-parameter video generation model, and at 143 GB, running it locally requires serious hardware. An NVIDIA B200 or multiple high-end GPUs.

The model is capable. MiniMax's hosted API generates competitive video. But downloading and running 143 GB of weights is impractical for most teams and impossible for hobbyists.

That's the gap Pruna targeted.

## How Pruna Shrunk It 7×

Pruna didn't just quantize H3. They rebuilt it for images. Here's what they changed, according to their [blog](https://www.pruna.ai/blog/oss-p-h3-image-launch) and model documentation:

**Prompt upsampler.** A new module based on Qwen3.5-9B that rewrites short user prompts into the kind of detailed captions H3 was trained on. Without it, short prompts produce mediocre results. With it, you type "a cat on a roof at sunset" and the model receives a paragraph-length description.

**Text encoder distillation.** The original 32-billion-parameter text encoder (67 GB) was distilled down to 4 billion parameters (9 GB). That single change wiped out almost half the original model's weight.

**Step reduction.** H3 used 50 diffusion steps. Pruna cut that to 8 using a technique they describe as distillation — no classifier-free guidance needed. Eight steps, one forward pass each. That's the speed multiplier.

**Backbone surgery.** The transformer backbone went from 33B to roughly 20B parameters. Pruna replaced the timestep conditioning block — about 40% of the transformer — with a smaller version that produces equivalent results.

<Callout type="info">
The Hugging Face model card still lists a "33.1B-parameter transformer" in its metadata, while the blog says 20B after the backbone change. This discrepancy exists in Pruna's own sources. The download sizes match the 20B claim, so the metadata likely describes the architecture template, not the active parameter count.
</Callout>

**Decoder rebuild.** H3's video decoder was replaced with a single-frame decoder optimized for still images.

**Quantization.** On top of all that, the final weights ship in bf16, 8-bit, and 4-bit variants.

## The Numbers

Here's what you actually need to run each variant, measured at 8 megapixels (the model's sweet spot for quality):

| Variant | Download Size | Peak VRAM (8 MP) |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| bf16 | 55 GB | ~56 GB |
| int8 | 30 GB | ~31 GB |
| w4a8 (4-bit) | 20 GB | ~21 GB |
| *Original H3* | *143 GB* | *Much more* |

These figures are from the [ComfyUI model card](https://huggingface.co/PrunaAI/P-H3-Image-Comfy) and cover the diffusion model, text encoder, and VAE — but not the prompt upsampler, which adds significant overhead depending on precision.

The diffusers pipeline with the upsampler included peaks at about 101 GB in bf16 at 8 MP. With CPU offloading, that drops to around 80 GB — tight for an 80 GB GPU like an H100, but workable.

### Speed

On a single NVIDIA B200 in bf16:

| Resolution | MiniMax-H3 (50 steps) | P-H3-Image (8 steps) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| 4 MP | 12.4 s | 2.6 s |
| 8 MP | 30.6 s | 5.8 s |

On a 48 GB NVIDIA L40S, the 4-bit ComfyUI set renders an image in 6.3 seconds. The bf16 set on the same card takes 19 seconds because it has to offload to system RAM.

Roughly 5× faster than the original. That's not a rounding trick — it's the compound effect of fewer steps, a smaller backbone, and quantized arithmetic.

## What It Can Do

**Text-to-image** at 2 to 16 megapixels. 8 MP produces the best results according to Pruna's documentation. Below that, quality drops.

**Image editing** with up to 8 reference images. The Hugging Face documentation says 8; one place in the blog says 9. Treat 8 as the reliable number.

**Resolution flexibility.** Multiple aspect ratios are supported at 8 MP.

**What doesn't work well:** Very short or hype-heavy prompts produce weaker output — the prompt upsampler helps, but it can only do so much with vague input.

## How to Run It

Two paths, both officially supported:

**Diffusers (Python).** Install the latest diffusers from GitHub and follow the usage example on the [PrunaAI/P-H3-Image model card](https://huggingface.co/PrunaAI/P-H3-Image). The pipeline supports bf16, int8, and 4-bit inference with optional CPU offloading.

**ComfyUI.** Download the weights from PrunaAI/P-H3-Image-Comfy. The model is natively supported on ComfyUI's master branch and ships with the next stable release (v0.44.0+). No custom nodes required. Workflow JSONs are included for text-to-image, editing, and LoRA variants.

<Callout type="info">
A community GGUF port also exists at [AtomicChat/P-H3-Image-GGUF](https://huggingface.co/AtomicChat/P-H3-Image-GGUF), offering Q8 down to Q3 quantizations. The Q8 variant is 21.7 GB and the Q3_K is 9 GB. Quality at lower quantization levels is unverified — with only 8 diffusion steps, each step carries more weight, so aggressive quantization may have a larger impact than usual.
</Callout>

## The License Problem

This is where the story gets complicated.

P-H3-Image inherits the [MiniMax H3 Community License](https://huggingface.co/PrunaAI/P-H3-Image/blob/main/LICENSE), and that license defines "Excluded Territories": the European Union, the United Kingdom, the Republic of Korea, and the United States of America.

The license applies worldwide *except* in those territories. If you're in the US, the EU, the UK, or South Korea, you cannot use the community license. You need a separate license from MiniMax.

This is not a minor footnote. The [license request form](https://platform.minimax.io/h3-license) exists, and MiniMax does accept inquiries from excluded-territory organizations. But as of this writing, the community license — the one you get when you download the weights — does not cover the United States.

### What Is Covered

- The distilled text encoder components and the Qwen3.5-9B-based prompt upsampler are Apache 2.0. Those are clear.
- The ComfyUI node code is MIT-licensed. Also clear.
- The model weights themselves — the core diffusion transformer, the decoder, the LoRA — are under the MiniMax H3 Community License.

### The Commercial Clause

Even in permitted territories, commercial products with more than $20 million in yearly revenue need written authorization from MiniMax. All commercial products must display "Powered by MiniMax H3."

<Callout type="warning">
If you're a US-based developer, the community license explicitly excludes you. Apply to MiniMax for a separate license before deploying anything — the restriction is on running the weights locally.
</Callout>

## Can It Run on a Mac?

Officially: not confirmed. Pruna's documentation targets CUDA. The ComfyUI quantized kernels support "NVIDIA Ampere or newer and AMD RDNA3/4." Apple isn't listed.

However, the [Hugging Face diffusers card](https://huggingface.co/PrunaAI/P-H3-Image) includes a code comment suggesting `device_map: "mps"` for Apple devices. And the community has been moving fast:

- A third-party ComfyUI wrapper called Phosphene [reported](https://x.com/PhospheneAI/status/2118402583207563764) running P-H3-Image on an M4 Max at about 5 minutes per 8 MP image, on Macs with 48 GB or more.
- Another user [reported](https://x.com/gitstatus001/status/2118325490985607225) about 10 minutes per image on a 48 GB Mac Studio, while waiting for a proper MLX port.
- No official MLX port exists yet.

A Mac with enough unified memory could theoretically hold the bf16 set (55 GB download, ~56 GB peak on NVIDIA). But those figures come from CUDA testing, not Metal. The quantized ComfyUI kernels are not built for Apple hardware, so the smallest VRAM path isn't available natively — though the community GGUF ports may offer a route.

Expect this to change. GGUF ports and the likelihood of an MLX conversion both point to Mac becoming viable. Just not today, not officially, and not fast.

## Who Should Care

**If you have an NVIDIA GPU with 24+ GB of VRAM** and you're in a permitted territory, this is immediately useful. The 4-bit set runs in about 21 GB of VRAM (plus overhead for the prompt upsampler), and Pruna's samples suggest the output quality holds up well for an 8-step pipeline.

**If you're building a product** that needs fast, high-resolution image generation and you're outside the excluded territories, the 8-step pipeline with no CFG is appealing. Single-digit seconds on a 48 GB L40S is commercially interesting.

**If you're on a Mac,** watch the community. The model can theoretically fit. The software isn't there yet. An official MLX or Metal-optimized build would change the equation overnight.

**If you're in the US,** the model is impressive but not deployable under the current license. Apply for a separate license from MiniMax, use the hosted API, or wait for the terms to change. Don't download the weights and assume you're covered — you're not.

## The Bigger Picture

What Pruna did here is genuinely clever. They took a video model's learned representations — the understanding of motion, spatial coherence, and scene composition that comes from training on video — and repurposed those priors for still images. Then they systematically reduced every dimension: encoder size, step count, backbone parameters, and numerical precision.

The result is a 7× size reduction and roughly 5× speed improvement. That's the kind of compression that makes local AI practical instead of theoretical.

The license is the anchor. MiniMax's territorial exclusions mean that the regions with the biggest AI developer communities — the US and EU — can't use the weights without a separate agreement. Whether that changes depends on MiniMax, not Pruna.

For now, P-H3-Image is a proof of concept for what aggressive model surgery can achieve. For developers in permitted territories with the right GPU, it's ready to use today. For everyone else, it's a preview of where local image generation is heading — once the legal framework catches up to the engineering.
