Seven Grok models. Four older Claude models. Four days apart. And a December 2 EU deadline that is exactly 30 days out.

On November 3, Anthropic extends its SynthID-Text watermark to older Claude models — Mythos 5, Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6, and Sonnet 4.6. These models shipped before Anthropic's initial watermark rollout and have been generating unwatermarked text since. That ends tomorrow.

Four days later, on November 7, xAI turns on watermarking across seven Grok models. API responses get a statistical fingerprint. On by default. Team admins can opt out per model, but the default is clear: watermarked.

The timing is hard to read as anything other than a race to the same regulatory finish line.

## The December 2 Deadline Nobody Can Ignore

The EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations took effect on August 2, 2026. But the regulation included a grandfathering provision: AI systems already on the market before that date get until **December 2, 2026** to comply with the marking and detection requirements.

That is 30 days from today.

Around 190 organizations signed the EU's Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-generated Content by the end of July. Anthropic was among them. The Code of Practice is technically voluntary, but the underlying Article 50 obligations are not — they are law.

The practical effect: if you shipped an AI model before August 2 and it generates text, you need to mark that output in a machine-detectable way by December 2. Watermarking is the most straightforward path to compliance. And both Anthropic and xAI are making their moves with a month to spare.

<Callout type="info">
**A further deadline looms on February 2, 2027** for watermark-detection interoperability — meaning detection tools from different providers will need to work across each other's watermarks. That one is going to be technically brutal. More on it below.
</Callout>

## What Anthropic Is Doing

Anthropic [announced SynthID-Text watermarking](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-text-watermark) on August 14, 2026, applied globally to new Claude models. The company was explicit about the reason: EU AI Act compliance.

The approach uses Google DeepMind's SynthID-Text method. During text generation, the model's sampling process is nudged — specific token choices are biased in patterns that are invisible to readers but statistically detectable with the right key. Anthropic says the watermark has no measurable impact on output quality.

The [November 3 expansion](https://mashable.com/tech/anthropic-expands-text-watermarking-to-older-claude-versions) covers the older Claude models that were exempt from the initial rollout. Anthropic's original announcement said the company was "working to add watermarking for those models as well" over the coming months. That timeline has now crystallized: Mythos 5, Opus 4.7, Opus 4.6, and Sonnet 4.6 all get watermarked starting tomorrow.

There is no opt-out. The watermark is global with no way to disable it.

The detection side remains locked down. Anthropic's watermark detection API is in private preview, available only to what the company calls "eligible groups" — regulators, law enforcement, media organizations, fact-checkers, researchers, educational institutions, and EU civil society organizations. Regular developers and businesses cannot detect watermarks in Claude's output yet.

## What xAI Is Doing

xAI's implementation hits on November 7 and covers seven models:

- grok-4.7
- grok-4.6
- grok-4.5
- grok-4.3
- grok-4.20-reasoning
- grok-4.20-non-reasoning
- grok-4.20-multi-agent

The [xAI documentation](https://docs.x.ai/developers/advanced-api-usage/watermarking), last updated October 31, describes the approach as statistical watermarking with no impact on quality or speed. Every API response includes an `x-watermark: true` header so downstream applications know what they are receiving.

The key difference from Anthropic: xAI gives team admins the ability to opt out on a per-model basis. Anthropic ships no opt-out at all. xAI defaults to on but lets enterprise customers make the final call. That design difference matters more than it looks — I will come back to it.

The watermark is embedded automatically and requires no code changes from developers.

## The OpenAI Gap

The elephant in the room remains OpenAI. Scott Aaronson built a working text watermark implementation at OpenAI in 2022. The leadership team decided not to ship it. As of November 2026, OpenAI still has not deployed text watermarking on any of its models.

OpenAI does use C2PA metadata for generated images and SynthID watermarks for audio output. For text, there is nothing. The company has stated it is "working to expand provenance measures" across modalities, including text, but no timeline has been given.

If OpenAI's models count as systems that were on the EU market before August 2 — and it is hard to argue they were not — the December 2 grandfathering deadline would likely apply to them. Text output from GPT models currently carries no machine-detectable provenance signal. That is a problem that gets worse every day it goes unaddressed.

<Callout type="warning">
**OpenAI's leadership decided against shipping text watermarks in 2022.** Four years later, Anthropic and xAI have both shipped them, and a legal mandate is 30 days away. Whatever product-risk calculus drove the original decision, the competitive landscape has inverted: not watermarking is now the outlier position.
</Callout>

## Why the Opt-Out Question Is a Bet on Enforcement

The xAI opt-out creates an interesting precedent. And I think Anthropic is making the wrong call by not offering one.

The EU AI Act's Article 50(2) requires providers to mark AI-generated content in a machine-detectable way. But not every AI output is the same. A grammar correction and a fully generated blog post sit at opposite ends of a spectrum, and enforcement will eventually need to deal with that spectrum.

By offering per-model opt-out, xAI is betting that enterprise customers operating in regulated environments will make reasonable decisions — and that the regulation will not punish providers for giving customers control. By refusing any opt-out, Anthropic is taking the most conservative posture possible. If enforcement turns out to be strict and literal, Anthropic is safe. If enforcement turns out to be nuanced, xAI's approach was the more honest design.

My bet: the nuanced reading wins. Blanket watermarking of every API call regardless of context will eventually create friction that the regulation did not intend — imagine a spell-checker watermarking its corrections. The companies that built escape hatches early will look prescient.

## Detection Is Still the Hard Part

Both companies claim their watermarks are robust. Both claim no quality impact. Those are the easy parts to ship.

The hard part is detection. Anthropic's detection API is in private preview with a narrow audience — regulators, researchers, journalists. xAI's documentation does not describe any public detection tooling. Neither company has published false positive rates, minimum token thresholds for reliable detection, or robustness data against paraphrasing and translation.

Then there is the February 2, 2027 deadline for watermark-detection interoperability under the EU AI Act. The regulation expects detection tools to work across providers — meaning a tool should be able to flag AI-generated content whether it came from Claude, Grok, or anything else. Cross-provider watermark detection, across different embedding schemes and model architectures, is a research problem that nobody has demonstrated publicly. And the deadline is three months away.

## What This Means for Developers

If you are building on Claude or Grok APIs, here is what changes:

**For Claude users**: Starting November 3, all Claude models produce watermarked output. There is nothing to configure and no way to turn it off. If you are building products that repackage Claude's output, your downstream users will be consuming watermarked text whether you disclose it or not. The detection API is not available to you yet, so you cannot verify watermarks yourself.

**For Grok users**: Starting November 7, watermarking is on by default. Check the `x-watermark: true` response header to confirm. If your use case requires unwatermarked output, your team admin can disable it per model. You should have a documented reason for doing so, particularly if you operate in the EU.

**For multi-model applications**: If you route between Claude, Grok, GPT, and other models, your output will be a mix of watermarked and unwatermarked text. There is currently no unified detection standard. The provenance story for your application is only as strong as its weakest link.

## The Bigger Picture

When Anthropic shipped text watermarking in August, it was easy to treat as one company's compliance move. Now xAI is following, with a different implementation and different opt-out rules, and a hard EU deadline is forcing the issue for everyone else.

Watermarking is becoming table stakes for AI providers operating in regulated markets. The December 2 grandfathering deadline will force every provider with EU exposure to either ship watermarking or explain why they have not. The February 2027 interoperability requirement will push toward standardization — or expose how far we are from it.

The question is no longer whether AI text will be watermarked. It is whether the detection infrastructure will be ready before the mandates assume it exists. Right now, the answer is no. Watermarks are being embedded faster than detection tools are being deployed. That gap is going to produce some uncomfortable regulatory moments in 2027.
