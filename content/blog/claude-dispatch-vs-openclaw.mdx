I woke up to a push notification on my phone: "Claude finished organizing your tax receipts." I hadn't touched my Mac in two hours.

That's Claude Dispatch — Anthropic's newest feature that turned my phone into a remote control for an AI agent running on my desktop. It launched on March 17, 2026, and it's already being called the thing that finally makes OpenClaw irrelevant.

But is it? I've been using both for the past 48 hours, and the answer is more nuanced than the headlines suggest.

## What Claude Dispatch Actually Is

Claude Dispatch creates a persistent bridge between your phone (Claude mobile app) and your desktop (Claude Desktop with Cowork). You scan a QR code, pair the devices, and suddenly you can send tasks from your couch, your commute, or your lunch break — and Claude executes them on your Mac.

The setup takes about two minutes. No API keys. No OAuth flows. No config files. Scan, pair, done.

Here's what happens under the hood: you type a prompt on your phone — something like "Find all PDFs in my Downloads folder from this month and rename them with the date prefix." Dispatch sends that instruction over an end-to-end encrypted channel to your desktop. Claude Desktop picks it up, executes it in the Cowork sandboxed environment, and sends back a confirmation with a summary of what it did.

The conversation persists across sessions. You can start a task in the morning, check progress at lunch, and refine the output in the evening — all from your phone.

## What OpenClaw Actually Is

For the uninitiated: OpenClaw is the open-source AI agent that took GitHub by storm. Created by Peter Steinberger (the PSPDFKit founder), it racked up 247,000 stars and has become the default choice for developers who want an always-on, local-first AI assistant.

OpenClaw runs on your machine — Mac, Windows, Linux, even a Raspberry Pi — and connects to your life through messaging apps. Telegram, WhatsApp, Discord, Signal, Slack, Microsoft Teams. You text your AI like you'd text a friend, and it executes tasks on your behalf.

It's model-agnostic, running Claude, GPT, DeepSeek, or completely local models on your own hardware. It has over 100 built-in skills, 3,000+ community extensions in the ClawHub marketplace, and a persistent memory system that stores your preferences as local Markdown files.

The killer feature: OpenClaw never sleeps. It runs 24/7, handling scheduled jobs, monitoring files, and proactively executing tasks even when you're not actively chatting with it.

## The Security Showdown

Anthropic has a genuine edge here.

### Claude Dispatch's Security Model

Anthropic built Dispatch with what they call "strict Human-in-the-Loop controls." In practice, this means:

- **End-to-end encryption** between your phone and desktop. The command data traveling between devices stays private — Anthropic can't read your instructions in transit.
- **Destructive action gates**: Before Claude deletes files, moves large directories, or performs any action flagged as potentially harmful, it pauses and sends a push notification to your phone asking for explicit approval.
- **Folder-level permissions**: Claude can only interact with folders and applications you've explicitly shared through Cowork. It can't silently expand its access.
- **Sandboxed execution**: Cowork runs tasks in Apple's Virtualization Framework on macOS. Even if something goes wrong, the blast radius is contained.

Anthropic's broader security track record backs this up. Their red team testing shows 96% prevention in tool-use scenarios, rising to 99.4% with additional safeguards like prompt shields. Their Claude Opus 4.5 system card runs 153 pages — compared to OpenAI's 60 pages for GPT-5 — suggesting a deeper investment in documenting and mitigating risks.

### OpenClaw's Security Model

OpenClaw gives you a choice: sandboxed mode or full system access. That flexibility is both its strength and its vulnerability.

In sandboxed mode, OpenClaw operates within defined boundaries. But the moment you grant full access — and most power users do, because that's where the real utility lives — you're trusting an open-source agent with the keys to your entire system.

The community has flagged this repeatedly. NVIDIA responded by building NemoClaw, a security-hardened fork specifically designed for enterprise environments. The fact that a major company felt the need to build a secure wrapper around OpenClaw tells you something about the default security posture.

OpenClaw stores configuration data and interaction history locally as Markdown files. That's great for privacy (nothing leaves your machine), but it also means there's no centralized security audit, no encrypted transport layer for those memories, and no built-in mechanism to prevent a compromised skill from reading sensitive files.

<Callout type="info">
The security difference isn't about which tool is "safer" in absolute terms. It's about defaults. Claude Dispatch defaults to locked-down with opt-in expansion. OpenClaw defaults to open with opt-in restrictions. Your risk tolerance determines which approach fits.
</Callout>

### The Numbers

Independent testing paints a stark picture of the broader ecosystem:

- Anthropic's injection prevention: 96% baseline, 99.4% with safeguards
- Fujitsu's independent assessment found GPT-5 vulnerable to approximately 20% of tested injection attacks
- Claude Code Security traces data flows rather than pattern-matching known threats, catching complex vulnerabilities that rule-based tools miss

OpenClaw doesn't publish equivalent security benchmarks, which is itself a data point.

## The Efficiency Comparison

Security aside, does Dispatch actually get things done better than OpenClaw?

### Where Dispatch Wins

**Seamless mobile-to-desktop handoff.** OpenClaw requires you to set up messaging integrations, configure bots, and maintain those connections. Dispatch is scan-and-go. The UX gap is enormous.

**Ecosystem integration.** If you're already using Claude Code, Claude Desktop, or Claude in Chrome, Dispatch slots into your existing workflow. The persistent conversation context means Claude remembers what you discussed in your coding session when you send a follow-up from your phone.

**Quality of execution.** Claude's underlying models (Opus 4.6, Sonnet 4.6) consistently outperform on complex reasoning tasks. On SWE-bench — the industry benchmark for real-world code changes — Claude scores 72.5% compared to OpenAI Codex's ~49%. That 23-point gap translates directly to task quality when you're delegating complex work. When I asked both tools to reorganize a messy project folder with nested dependencies, Claude got the structure right on the first pass. OpenClaw with GPT-4o needed two corrections.

### Where OpenClaw Wins

**Always-on operation.** This is OpenClaw's biggest advantage — and it's not close. Dispatch requires your Mac to stay awake with Claude Desktop running. Close the lid, and Claude stops. OpenClaw runs as a background service, handling scheduled jobs and monitoring tasks around the clock. Deploy it on a Raspberry Pi, and it truly never sleeps.

**Model flexibility.** Dispatch locks you into Claude models. OpenClaw lets you pick the best model for each task — Claude for complex reasoning, a local Llama model for private tasks, GPT for specific use cases. Model-agnostic means you're never trapped.

**Extensibility.** OpenClaw's 3,000+ community extensions dwarf anything in the Claude ecosystem. Need a skill that monitors your stock portfolio? Someone built it. Want to automate your smart home? There's an extension for that. This long tail of niche capabilities is where open-source thrives.

**Cost.** OpenClaw is free. You pay only for API calls to cloud models, and you can run entirely local models for zero ongoing cost. Dispatch requires a Claude Max subscription at $100-200/month minimum. That's a massive price gap for individual users.

**Platform support.** OpenClaw runs everywhere — Mac, Windows, Linux. Dispatch is Mac-only today. If you're on Windows or Linux, the decision is already made for you.

<Callout type="warning">
Dispatch is currently in research preview and Anthropic reports approximately 50% reliability on task completion. It also struggles with app launching and messaging. These are early-stage limitations, but they matter if you need something dependable today.
</Callout>

## The Bigger Picture: Agent Architectures in 2026

Two competing visions for AI agents are playing out in real time.

**Anthropic's vision: The walled garden.** Claude Dispatch, Cowork, Claude Code, Claude in Chrome, and the Agent SDK form a tightly integrated ecosystem. Everything talks to everything. Security is enforced at every layer. The trade-off is vendor lock-in and premium pricing.

**OpenClaw's vision: The open protocol.** Anyone can build skills. Any model can power it. Any messaging platform can serve as the interface. The trade-off is fragmented security and "works on my machine" reliability.

The Claude Agent SDK sits somewhere in between. It gives developers the same tools that power Claude Code — file reading, bash execution, web search, code editing — in a programmable package. You can build custom agents with Python or TypeScript, define exactly which tools they can use, and enforce permission boundaries programmatically. It's Anthropic's answer to developers who want Claude's capabilities without the full Claude Desktop experience.

Here's what the SDK looks like in practice:

```python
from claude_agent_sdk import query, ClaudeAgentOptions

async for message in query(
    prompt="Find and fix the bug in auth.py",
    options=ClaudeAgentOptions(allowed_tools=["Read", "Edit", "Bash"]),
):
    print(message)
```

That's the entire agent loop. Claude reads the file, finds the bug, edits it — using the same engine that powers Claude Code but in a form you can embed in your own applications.

## So Is Dispatch the OpenClaw Killer?

No. And framing it that way misses the point.

Dispatch and OpenClaw serve overlapping but different audiences:

**Choose Claude Dispatch if:**
- You're already in the Claude ecosystem
- Security and sandboxing are non-negotiable
- You want a polished, zero-configuration mobile experience
- You're willing to pay premium pricing for premium guardrails
- You're on macOS

**Choose OpenClaw if:**
- You want always-on, background agent capabilities
- Model flexibility matters more than ecosystem integration
- You're price-sensitive or want to run fully local
- You need Windows or Linux support
- You value community extensibility over curated experiences

**Choose both if:**
- You're a developer who uses Claude Code for work and wants OpenClaw for personal automation
- You need different security postures for different tasks
- You appreciate that no single agent does everything well

The real question isn't which one kills the other. It's whether AI agents are mature enough to be trusted with real tasks at all. Dispatch's 50% reliability and OpenClaw's security-by-opt-in both suggest we're still in the early innings.

## What I'm Actually Using

I've settled into a workflow that uses both. Claude Dispatch handles my work tasks — organizing project files, synthesizing research from Notion, processing documents — because I want the sandboxed execution and the audit trail. OpenClaw handles my personal automation — home monitoring, message routing, scheduled reminders — because it runs 24/7 and I can use local models for privacy.

The split happened naturally. I tried running everything through Dispatch for a week — and hit a wall every time my Mac went to sleep or I needed a task to run at 3 AM. I tried running everything through OpenClaw — and got uncomfortable when I realized a community skill I'd installed could read my work documents without any permission gate.

So I drew a line: anything touching client data goes through Claude's sandboxed pipeline. Everything else goes through OpenClaw. It's not elegant, but it works.

Neither is perfect. Dispatch is too new and too unreliable for mission-critical work. OpenClaw is too open and too unaudited for anything touching sensitive data.

But together? They cover a lot of ground. Last Tuesday, Dispatch organized my client project files while OpenClaw monitored my home security cameras and sent me a Telegram alert when a package arrived. Two agents, two jobs, zero overlap. That's the actual future — not one tool to rule them all, but the right tool for each context.

<Callout type="tip">
If you're choosing between the two, start with the one that matches your existing tools. Already using Claude? Try Dispatch. Already comfortable with Docker and self-hosting? Try OpenClaw. The switching cost is low, and the best agent is the one you'll actually use.
</Callout>

## Guardrails vs. Freedom — What It Comes Down To

Claude Dispatch is more secure. OpenClaw is more flexible. Dispatch is polished but limited. OpenClaw is powerful but rough.

Anthropic built a remote control. The open-source community built a Swiss Army knife. Which one you need depends on whether you value guardrails or freedom.

Here's what I think most people miss: we're not choosing between two finished products. Dispatch has 50% reliability and zero Windows support. OpenClaw has 247,000 GitHub stars and no security audit. Both are bets on where this space is headed, not where it is.

The smarter move isn't picking a winner. It's learning both paradigms — the locked-down enterprise model and the open-source extensible model — because six months from now, the tools will be different but the architectures will be the same. And the people who understand both will be the ones building the next wave of agents, not just using them.
